Use the kit to start a new product. Use this when you're testing a concept,
building a prototype, or standing up something that doesn't yet have a team
or a roadmap.

## 2. Look at how it's been done before

Check the kit site before you start. You're looking for how other teams
have approached and styled the thing you're about to make, not only for a
component with the right name.

Look for:

- products that have solved a similar problem, and what they did
- the patterns and layouts the kit already sets out
- decisions and deviation records covering the ground you're on

Someone has usually been here before. Starting from what they landed on is
faster than starting from a blank page, and it means your prototype is
comparable with everything else.

## 3. Automate making it with the i.AI cookie cutter or AI agent skill

### i.AI cookie cutter
Start from the i.AI cookie cutter. It comes with the components, styles and
best practice already in place, so you don't have to assemble any of it
yourself. That's the point of it: the parts that are already solved are
handed to you, and your time goes on the new and innovative parts that only
you can work out.

Don't build a component the kit already has, even for a throwaway
prototype. Prototypes become products more often than anyone plans for.

### AI Agent skill:
If an agent is doing the build, point it at the kit before it writes
anything. See also
[use the kit in your code](/get-started/journey-3-apply-the-kit-to-an-existing-product#use-the-kit-in-your-code).

## 4. Build it and Check it

At prototype stage, check that the behaviour works and that a person other
than you has looked at it. Get support with getting it infront of users to get insights.

A full accessibility check isn't required until the work leaves prototype
stage. Record that you haven't done one, so nobody assumes it has been.

## 5. Contribute what you built to the kit

Anyone in i.AI can contribute; use agent to package it up and raise a PR.
